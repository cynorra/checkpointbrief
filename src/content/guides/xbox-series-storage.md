**Xbox Series X|S games can only be played from the internal SSD or an official Storage Expansion Card. A USB drive can store them, but you have to move them back to play.** Older Xbox One, Xbox 360 and original Xbox games can play straight from USB. That split decides what to buy ([Windows Central's storage guide](https://www.windowscentral.com/xbox-series-x-s-storage-guide)).

The expansion card uses Xbox's own storage architecture, so it behaves exactly like the internal drive. Seagate makes the official card, in 512 GB, 1 TB and 2 TB sizes, and other licensed cards have followed. Look for the official "Designed for Xbox" branding: an ordinary SSD won't fit the slot.

## The smart way to combine them

Many players use both:

1. Keep the games you're playing now on the internal drive or expansion card.
2. Move everything else, especially big Series X|S games you'll come back to, to a cheap USB drive.
3. When you want one back, move it from USB to internal. Copying over USB is usually much faster than redownloading it.

To move a game: press the Xbox button, go to **My games & apps → Manage**, choose a storage device and select **Move or copy**.

## Common mistakes

- **Buying a regular SSD and expecting it to fit.** The expansion slot needs an official, licensed Xbox card.
- **Expecting a USB drive to play Series X|S games.** It can only store them.
- **Forgetting that the drive formats when you connect it.** Setting a USB drive up for Xbox erases it, so copy any files off first.
- **Letting storage fill up completely.** Leave some free space on the internal drive for updates and temporary files.

## Checking space on the Xbox

Press the Xbox button, then go to **My games & apps → Manage → Storage devices** (menu names can change with updates). It shows how much space each drive has and which games use the most. Sort by size and look at what you haven't played in months.
